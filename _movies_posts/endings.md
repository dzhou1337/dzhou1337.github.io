# Endings
#### last updated 2026/10/28

The endings of movies are not particularly important. If you've ever watched a movie and said you enjoyed it all up until the ending ruined everything, you are lying. There is no movie out there with an ending so profound and powerful that it ripped through the fabric of time to find your past self enjoying the movie and rewrote the joy out of your experience.

We don't watch movies to find out what happens at the end. As film philosopher Nicole Kidman puts it, we go to the movies to laugh, to cry, to care. The most important part of a movie is not how narratively sound the ending is, how well it wraps things up. It's how you felt and what you experienced through the course of watching the movie. The visual splendor, the emotional investment -- it's everything you experience in the moment. This isn't anything that can be taken from you. What you felt is what you felt, and nothing can undo what you felt.

Obviously, endings can be bad and they are part of the movie-watching experience. Obviously they disproportionately affect how you reflect on the movie. It's the last thing you see before you're plopped back into the real world. But it's just so disingenuous to act like a bad ending discounts everything else.

"It was all a dream" is a common trope of a bad ending, but why? The correct answer is that it's played out and uncreative. The Real answer is people don't like being invested in something where the outcome never mattered. "What do you mean it was all a dream? You mean all that stuff I cared about never happened? The stakes were never real?". Come on. YOU'RE WATCHING A MOVIE. You suspend your disbelief to be invested in a made-up story, and then the made-up story tells you it was all a made-up story, and now you're upset?

At the end of the day, it's the end of the day. If you get everything done that you wanted to get done, it's a good day. If the movie made me feel something, it's a good movie. 🥀
