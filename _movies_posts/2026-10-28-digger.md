# Digger (2026) Review 
#### 2026/10/28

![digga](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/7/71/Digger_video_game_cover.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)

There's levels to this.

'Digger' is ostensibly about climate change. Our protagonist is the eponymous Digger, an oil baron who is cartoonishly evil, greedy, and stupid. I've seen one description of him that seems quite apt: imagine Elon Musk crossed with Yosemite Sam. A caricature of a caricature of how we see our modern billionaire overlords. Our first impression of Digger is him refusing to consider the risks of a business venture that is literally about to end the world for the sake of drilling more oil. It's a completely unsubtle analogy for how our own mighty industry leaders mortgage the health of the natural world for the sake of satiating us good capitalist consumers.

But really, 'Digger' is not about climate change. Climate change is more of a backdrop for the real story that 'Digger' is telling, which is the story that Digger is telling (please follow along with the intentional use and non-use of single quotes here). Digger isn't actually a real person in the canon of 'Digger'. Well, he is, but we don't actually ever see the real Digger in 'Digger'. Instead, we're presented with an actor who is playing Digger. This actor isn't Tom Cruise, it's the actor who Cruise is playing, who is named Curtis, who is playing Digger in a story about Digger in the movie 'Digger' called Fossil Fools. 

Put more simply, 'Digger' is about the production of a play called Fossil Fools. We see the narrative focus of 'Digger' jump between the story of Digger and the story of Curtis. At first, it's jarring and maybe even annoying to be pulled out of the fun drama of Digger's story and dropped into Curtis's story. Digger is larger than life and gets up to entertainingly despicable schemes. Curtis is an old dad worried about his daughter not calling him. Yawn.

So why aren't we just focused on Digger's story? If the movie were just the parts where we're seeing the performance of Fossil Fools, that would still be a decently enjoyable movie. There are charming practical effects that get bonus points for fitting in with the stipulation that Fossil Fools is a stageplay. The most comedic and bombastic acting performances are contained in the Fossil Fools scenes as well. You'd probably just end up with a more entertaining version of 'Don't Look Up' if that's how 'Digger' was drawn up.

That's not the movie we got though. 'Digger' needs the meta-narrative to convey what it's really about. Digger was a real man in the canon of 'Digger', and Fossil Fools is a play that points and laughs at him. Digger did actually destroy the world, and now the people who inherit the ruins get to use his dramatized story as a source of black comedy. It's like their version of making 9/11 jokes and making funny caricatures of George Dubya. The twist is that Digger's nephew, inheritor of Digger's wealth, is the rights holder to Fossil Fools and is dumping an unlimited budget into its production.

'Digger' is not a critique of billionaires who are destroying the world. It's not a critique of billionaires who control global narratives. It's not a critique of billionaires at all. It's a critique of critiques of billionaires.

Critiquing billionaires for destroying the world is like critiquing carbon dioxide for warming the globe. That's just what they do, it's in their nature.

Fossil Fools is a critique of billionaires, and it's a tool that little baby-Digger wields to pacify his audience with laughter. He controls the narrative and gives the hoi polloi a controlled outlet for their disdain for Digger, and thus their disdain for the powers that be that destroyed their world (and presumably continue to destroy it).

If you got some sense of catharsis when Digger killed himself, 'Digger' is targeted at you. You sat there and watched a movie that cost hundreds of millions of dollars to make. Dollars spent by those aforementioned billionaires for the sake of making a diggillion dollars in return (the fact that it's lost hundreds of millions is just a hilarious coincidence). If you let this movie pacify your justified hunger for billionaire blood, if even for a moment, you are living proof that media like Fossil Fools is an effective tool for quieting the righteous anger of the masses.

You think Warner Brothers cares about you? You think anything they distribute would meaningfully harm the billionaire class? You're never gonna buy a product that's gonna bring about the revolution if that product came out of the brutal machine run by your oppressors. There's no film, book, song, or movie review that's going to lift you out from servitude to your masters. And they just threw away 300 million dollars to flaunt that message in your face.
