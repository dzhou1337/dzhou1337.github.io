# 2026 LCS Matchups

## ALCS: Tampa Bay Rays v Cleveland Guardians

## NLCS: Los Angeles Dodgers v Milwaukee Brewers


What's with all these poverty teams in the playoffs? Somebody get these BEGGARS out of here. You're telling me three out of the four best teams in baseball live in towns like Cleveland and Tampa Bay? When I watch playoff sports, I'm here to watch the biggest, highest-paid names, from the biggest, highest cost-of-living cities. Guys who would adorn the cover of Sports Illustrated. Guys like Aaron Judge, Bryce Harper, (the list ends here because MLB does not have enough relevance today to have household names). At least Shohei Ohtani's shambling corpse made it in (batting .100 for the last 3 months with no homers).

It's frankly a poor message to be sending to American families in this day and age. To think that you can sniff the pinnacle of your rat race of a field on a shoestring budget is maddening. The Dodgers know how to run a baseball team, as evidenced by the $340 million they're spending on payroll this year. Add up the payroll for the Guardians, Rays, and Brewers, and you barely end up with $315 million combined. These teams are putting 3x less money (effort) into their teams compared to LA, yet they have gotten just as far. What does this tell us? It tells us MLB is promoting un-American values.

The greatest American value is the pursuit, spending, and hoarding of our store of value, the USD. Clearly the likes of the Dolan family, owners of the Guardians, have embodied the hoarding value, but where is the spending? Don't excuse such miserly behavior on their less-than-modest town of origin. Every MLB owner is a multi-billionaire and can afford to spend as much as the Guggenheim Group spends on the Dodgers.

See the example set by these penny-pinching teams. The Dodgers are spending $50 million on star right-fielder Kyle Tucker, a 4x All-Star, 2x Silver Slugger winner, and 2022 World Series champion (and he's 6'3). The Brewers' right-fielder? Some 5'9 schmuck of no renown from Chestnut Hill, MA named Sal Frelick with barely an accolade to his name. And how much do the Brewers pay for his services? Not even $1 million. Both outfielders have produced approximately the same amount of on-field value this last season. This is instilling a toxic idea in the American consumer: spending more money doesn't get you more value.

Year over year, Americans dwarf every other nation in consumer spending, accounting for 30% of the world's consumer spending despite only making up 4% of the world's population. What would happen if US consumers spent less like the Dodgers and spent more like the Brewers? What would happen if US consumers let some other third world country overtake them in spending? What would happen to our national identity if we let that happen?

I'll pose one more question: who's more important, the person with the most money, or the person who spends the most money? I don't care if you sit on a mountain of gold if I can't get any of it from you. I'd rather hang out with the suburban father of three who would buy me a drink over the billionaire who would ask to split the bill. Who gives a fuck if the US has the highest GDP, net-worth, quality of life etc. if people stop spending money? Why does the world cater to our every whim? Because the US is that guy buying everyone drinks.
